## 2. What is done well
- **One place for authorisation** (API `policy.ts`), with an explicit role × category × grant-state negative test matrix and Postgres RLS planned as a second layer.
- **The senior owns all grants.** Grants are per category and per grantee, **always expire** (default 90 days, max 365), and new members start with zero grants. Caregivers are hard-excluded from scam and transfer data.
- **Grants are re-checked at delivery**, so a revoke between enqueue and send stops the notification. Notification bodies are minimal.
- **Honest vocabulary by construction.** There is no `verified_emergency`, no "safe" risk level, no "connected" call state, and alert origin separates `system_warning` from `user_requested`. Missing data is neutral, never shown as decline. "Check In Recommended" comes only from explicit shared signals.
- **Rules-first ScamShield.** The AI is off by default, can only raise the level, input is treated as data, and output is validated against a schema. **No server-side URL fetching** (T6). Help numbers come from the directory, never from AI.
- **Strong exclusions**: no location, call recording, SMS/LINE reading, emotion inference or diagnosis.
- Simulated providers for notifications and payments, and a three-part condition before any live payment provider. Synthetic data only. B4/B5 are honestly flagged as unverified.
- Admin is operational only, with no content endpoints. B2B stats require k ≥ 10.

## 3. Verdict

**ARCH gate: recommend approve with conditions.**

The architecture (separate API policy engine, consent-grant ABAC, transactional queue with grant re-check, rules-floor AI, simulated adapters) is sound. The contract, however, has authorisation and coercion gaps that the parallel builders will otherwise build in.

Conditions for signing ARCH:
1. SR-01 to SR-13 are resolved **in `packages/shared/src/api.ts` / `domain.ts` and in threat model v0.2**, and the lead approves the contract change. Builders of `services/api` and `apps/web` must be told **now**, because code is being written against the current contract.
2. A **named human security reviewer** is appointed and co-signs (B1). This agent review alone does not satisfy the gate's independence requirement.
3. The SR-14 to SR-24 Medium items are tracked as QA-gate conditions. SR-16 additionally blocks UAT. SR-19 and SR-34 block enabling any AI provider.
4. SR-31 to SR-34 (PDPA) block any real-user pilot, not the synthetic MVP.

## 4. PDPA: what a qualified reviewer must confirm (not legal advice)
A qualified Thai data-protection lawyer or DPO must confirm:
1. Whether self-reported mood and well-being check-ins and health reminders are **health data under PDPA s.26**, and so need explicit consent. Also the lawful basis for scam-check and Guardian Pause data (consent, contract or legitimate interest).
2. Whether per-grant consent text plus an account-level consent satisfies the requirements for explicit, specific, freely given and withdrawable consent, given the **power imbalance inside families**.
3. Consent by or for older adults with reduced capacity (curators and guardians under the Civil and Commercial Code, PDPA s.20) and by minors (under 20).
4. Processing the data of non-user third parties (trusted contacts, people named in scam messages) and the privacy notice they need.
5. Whether a **DPO is required** (s.41: core activity involving sensitive data), and the RoPA (s.39), security-measure standards (PDPC notifications), breach notification within 72 h (s.37(4)) and processor agreements (s.40).
6. **Cross-border transfer** (s.28/29 and the related PDPC notifications) if the Anthropic API, push providers or a non-Thai staging region is used.
7. Whether the retention periods (12 months for check-ins, 30 days for raw scam text, 2 years for audit and revoked grants) are justified, and how they interact with deletion requests.
8. Whether the "who viewed my info" log and family alerts create any new obligations toward family members as data subjects.

The section numbers above come from secondary sources and the reviewer must verify them.
